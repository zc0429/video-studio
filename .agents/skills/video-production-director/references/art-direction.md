# Art direction: evidence, decisions and review / v1.4

Load this reference when selecting a look or reviewing images/motion. It defines a working method, not a universal aesthetic theory or a guarantee that the film is beautiful. Do not add a mandatory user approval: delegated creative decisions remain delegated.

## 1. Build a small working set

Start from the film's premise, audience, emotional change and output format. In the full repository, scan `aesthetics/INDEX.json` for relevant entries; initially read one primary visual reference and at most one complementary card. This is a context-management default, not an artistic limit. Expand only to answer a named unresolved question. Read only relevant entries from `aesthetics/PREFERENCES.json`; don't load every previous project, the full SOP/PDF or all case studies by default.

Standalone skill users can research primary sources or use authorized local references instead of the repository library. Check the actual image/clip and source identity. If external sources are inaccessible, use a different available source or explicitly develop an original visual hypothesis with a self-reviewed study; do not invent a reference or stop solely to obtain another permission for an already delegated decision.

## 2. Distinguish what each source supports

Record source URL/file, creator, title, exact object/shot ID, access date, inspection method and reuse basis. Keep these separate:

- **Observed:** something actually visible or audible in inspected material.
- **Interpretation:** our explanation of its effect, which can be debated.
- **Project hypothesis:** how a principle might serve this new story.
- **Test evidence:** our exported frames, motion preview, actual inspection and findings.

A museum attribution/description is sourced evidence, not an experiment proving viewer response. A static painting or sampled still supports composition/light analysis, not a claim that its editing, sound or motion was watched. Uninspected links stay leads. A popular video does not establish artistic quality or profitability. Never give an AI-generated score as proof of quality.

## 3. Translate the chosen reference into an original look

Use `assets/STYLE_BIBLE.md` as the project's single look document. State why the primary reference fits this premise, what to borrow at the level of principle, what to change, and which tempting elements to reject. Don't average several incompatible styles or copy a complete composition/character/sequence merely because its source is respected.

| Decision | Questions to resolve in this film | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning and visual hierarchy | What should the viewer notice first, then discover? Does the image express this beat? | Small/large preview, storyboard and actual frame |
| Composition and depth | How do scale, edges, occlusion and quiet areas guide attention? | Subject silhouette and foreground/middle/background relationships |
| Color and light | What changes with the emotion? Where are the brightest and most saturated areas? | Key frames across the arc, highlight/shadow continuity |
| Material and identity | Do surface response, proportions and lighting remain believable in this chosen medium? | Target-resolution details and neighboring frames |
| Typography | Is it readable and appropriately paced, with intentional placement and project-specific clearance? | Full-resolution text entry, hold and exit; not just a clean title frame |
| Camera, movement and editing | What narrative change motivates the movement/cut/hold? Does movement show progress? | Timed animatic and contiguous motion, not stills alone |
| Sound | What establishes space, emphasis, silence and emotional change? | Actual audition when possible, with the inspection scope recorded |

These questions are a project review rubric, not scientific universal rules. No fixed golden ratio, palette, constant camera movement, fast-cut rate or typography style is imposed. A justified quiet hold can work; an accidental frozen render cannot pass as a deliberate choice. 60fps output remains the repository's delivery requirement, not an aesthetic score.

## 4. Validate before expanding production

Create a few original style frames covering meaningful changes, rather than many near-identical variants. Compare them against the selected principles and one another. Record why the chosen direction serves the story; keep a rejected alternative only if it explains a real tradeoff. Review internally under the user's delegation.

Then make a full-length low-cost animatic with the intended information order, approximate narration/sound and actual pauses. A beautiful still does not pass a confusing sequence. Test the hardest motion/material/transition locally before expanding. Log a specific failure and a primary correction variable; return to story, layout, light or tool choice as appropriate instead of adding more stylistic adjectives or random seeds.

## 5. Review the produced film and learn from final feedback

Use `assets/ART_REVIEW.md`. Record artifact paths/time ranges, what was actually viewed/heard, observed problem, its impact and the smallest repair. Findings use `keep / revise / unverified`; do not manufacture a numeric beauty score. Separate measurable defects from aesthetic judgments. Technical or factual defects are repaired; deliberate artistic departures are explained against the premise and reference, not rejected automatically.

Review shot starts/middles/ends, transitions, peak motion, text timing and adjacent frames. Make a continuity pass and a separate story/emotion pass; this is a review procedure, not a request to spawn subagents. When required evidence cannot be inspected, say exactly what remains unverified; metadata tests do not fill that gap.

Final user delivery stays concise: the film, a lightweight preview, brief rationale and material limitations. Keep the detailed look and review evidence in the project for follow-up. The user can request micro-adjustments at final review; don't make them fill a style form first.

Use final feedback to update scoped preference entries with the user's actual words and project context. Approval of one whole film does not prove preference for every color, camera move or genre in it. One rejection does not create a ban for all future work. New lessons start as candidates; merge duplicates and retire contradicted/obsolete entries. Change the core skill only when a durable procedure changes.
